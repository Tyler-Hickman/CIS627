# CIS627
- Project Overview:
A useful analytics tool could be derived to help NFL coaches decide when to attempt a 4th down conversion. By using available in-game data regarding yards gained or lost on each play, and whether it was a run or pass play, coaches can determine how successful they may be attempting a 4th down conversion. 
- Decision-Making Problem:
This tool can help any and all NFL coaches lead their teams to a higher win percentage by having some degree of certainty in whether or not they will perform well on 4th down. More wins means more team success, more viewership, more TV deals, and more money into the team as a whole. 
- Proposed Analytics Approach:
Every time a play is ran by the offense, the yardage and success rate will be tracked, as well as the type of play that took place. Throughout the game, as more plays happen and more data is collected, the team will have more certainty as to whether or not they should decide to risk gaining the required 4th down yardage or turn the ball over. 
- Use by Decision Makers:
NFL coaches will have real-time access to this tool to aid in their decision-making on the fly. Knowing what choice to make in-game could be the difference between winning and losing. 
- Connection to Chapter 7:
Using Alamar's 4 phases of innovation, this concept exists within the engagement phase (Alamar, 2024). This tool exists, and can. be useful to coaches across the league. Those wishiing to invest in it would see benefit, but there isn't a whole lot to expand upon once the data is accessed.

- Prototype Enhancement:
In addition to utilizing in-game yards gained or lost on either run or pass plays, the tool will also track the success of similar plays across other games. By using historical data, the team can determine which plays are best for specific scenarios to minimize uncertainty. 
- Prototype Evaluation: 
The proposed prototype enhancement should be integrated into the main branch. The coaches and team decision makers should have access to historical data to aid in making informed real-time decisions.

Reflection on Innovation and Version Control:
Branches support low-risk integration of new code by ensuring that no accidental updates are committed. When a branch is made, a copy of the existing data can be edited without actually altering the existing data. If the new branch edits are confirmed to be of value, approving authorities can verify by signing off and ultimately committing those changes to the new branch. This sequence minimizes the risk of deletion or unintentional alteration of important code, while ensuring the changes are of value to the organization. 

Github helps analytics ideas gain traction among decision makers because of the inherent collaborative nature of branches. When multiple ideas are considered and a concept is up in the air, members from different teams with radical ideas can initiate their own branch to make potential changes. The best ideas are selected and committed and the process continues, weeding out any concepts that wouldn't succeed or may not be the best for the organization. This collaboration of ideas and solidification of only the best concepts allows for transparency for decision makers, ensuring that they know that only the best concepts get committed to the main branch.

This fits into Alamar's (2024) concepts of innovation because each of the four phases can be applied in Github. The first phase, the Creative phase, is the initial gathering of ideas. This can be accomplished by members creating their own branches to experiment with. Next, the Protype phase can take place, where each branch is tested and developed to reach a result. Once these are narrowed down and fine-tuned, they can be submitted to leadership authority for approval to commit to the main branch. This corresponds to the third concept of innovation, Engagement. Finally, in the Build phase, the ideas can be committed to the main branch and integrated into the system for completion. Github allows for all four phases of innovation, step by step.
